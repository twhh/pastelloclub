Nobody warns you that baby sleep advice is a contact sport. One book says strict routine, another says follow their lead, your aunt says rice cereal, and the internet says all of the above are wrong. Meanwhile you're standing in the nursery at 3 a.m. wondering if the grunting is normal. (It is. Mostly.)

We bought into plenty of theories, and we bought plenty of gear. Some of it earned a permanent spot on the nightstand, and the rest got returned, regifted, or buried in the closet next to the wipe warmer. This is the short list of what stayed - the five things that quietly made sleep easier without promising any miracles.

To be clear, none of these will make your baby sleep through the night tonight. But they remove the obstacles between your baby and the sleep they're already capable of, and when you're running on fumes, "removes obstacles" is the whole ballgame.

## A Sound Machine That Runs the Whole Bedtime

Here's the irony nobody mentions: silence is the enemy of baby sleep. Babies spend nine months in a womb that sounds like a running faucet, so a quiet room means every door creak and dog bark is a wake-up event. A good [sound machine](https://amzn.to/4vcvuF8) turns your thin-walled house back into muffled static.

The [Hatch sound machine and night light](https://amzn.to/4vcvuF8) is the one that stuck for us, because it's three tools in one. It plays the white noise, it's the night light for those half-asleep diaper changes, and the app lets you program it all - warm light and sound at bedtime, dimmer overnight, gentle light for morning. Adjusting any of it from your phone at the door means no creaky floorboards, no touching anything in the baby's room.

Honest note: the first week, you'll lie there wondering how anyone could sleep to that noise. Give it two weeks, and suddenly silence is what keeps you up.

## A Second Sleep Space That Earns Its Floor Space

Naps don't always happen in the nursery. Sometimes they happen in your room while you tiptoe around, at grandma's house, or in whatever corner of the house the toddler isn't currently thundering through. That's the case for a [Newton Deluxe Travel Crib](https://amzn.to/4z4BrWA) - and one non-negotiable accessory.

Get the bassinet insert. It raises the sleep surface to the top so you're not lowering a newborn to the floor at 2 a.m., and it transforms the whole thing from travel gear into a legitimate second crib. We set ours up in another room for daytime naps, and it saved the nursery for real nights.

Then there's the trick nobody told us: with the insert in, there's usable space underneath. When baby number two arrived, the older sibling would play or even nap below while the baby slept on top - same footprint, two kids handled. A travel crib that solves naps, travel, and sibling logistics at once is a rare thing.

## The Sleepsuit for the Flailing-Arms Stage

Around three or four months, the swaddle stops working. Your baby's startle reflex is still going strong, but they're rolling or breaking out, so arms-out is the new normal - which means every startled arm flap becomes a self-inflicted wake-up. This stage is brutal, and it's exactly what the [Merlin's Magic Sleepsuit](https://amzn.to/4AEUTuR) was built for.

Think of it as a gently padded suit that muffles the flail. The arms are free but the reflex doesn't whip them into a full jazz-hands wake-up, and for us it turned forty-minute naps into real ones. It was amazing once our baby was a little older - the sweet spot is after the swaddle but before confident rolling.

Fair warning: your baby will look like a very contented astronaut, and the suit is warm, so dress them lighter underneath and keep the room cool. It's also a stage product, not a forever one - once they can roll in it, it's time to graduate to a regular sleep sack.

## The Same Three Books Every Night

The final piece isn't gear so much as ritual, because babies can't read a clock - they read cues. A short stack of [touch and feel bedtime books](https://amzn.to/4hrBLJ2) became the cue that told our baby's body the day was ending, and it worked better than any schedule we tried to impose.

The touch-and-feel part matters more than you'd think. A six-month-old won't sit still for a story, but they will sit still to feel the fuzzy bunny, which keeps them on your lap long enough for the wind-down to actually happen. Then the repetition does the rest: same books, same order, same last page, every night. It's Pavlovian, and it's beautiful.

Keep them in a basket next to the feeding chair so the ritual has a home. They will be chewed on. That's proof of use, not damage.

## Sleep Is a Stage, Not a Setting

If there's one thing to hold onto, it's this: baby sleep isn't a problem you solve once. It's a series of stages you adapt to - the newborn grunts, the four-month regression, the flailing-arm era, the night they finally sleep long enough that you wake up in a panic checking the monitor. Every regression ends. Every stage passes.

So don't buy everything at once. Start with the sound machine and one book tonight, add the rest as the stages arrive. And someday, standing in a silent nursery at 3 a.m. because a teenager stayed out late, you may even miss the grunting.
