Ask about strollers at a baby store with a first kid on the way, and the same sentence arrives from every salesperson and forum thread: if you're planning two kids, get the Vista. The Cruz is the one-and-done stroller. The Vista is the family.

We researched both to death, stood in the store aisle for an hour, and bought the Cruz anyway - on purpose, with a plan for the second kid that didn't involve the Vista's double configuration. Four years and two kids later, here's the whole decision, receipts included.

The one-line versions:

- **Cruz:** UPPAbaby's full-size single stroller. Lighter, cheaper, one seat, done.
- **Vista:** the flagship. Heavier, pricier, ships with a bassinet, and converts to a double when kid two arrives.

## The Table I Built Before Choosing

Vista numbers are from research and spec sheets - we never owned one. We bought the Cruz V2 in 2022; stores sell the V3 now, so the table uses current V3 specs. [Our four-year review covers the V2 we actually own](/posts/review-uppababy-cruz/).

| | Cruz V3 | Vista V3 |
|---|---|---|
| Price (2026) | ~$700s | ~$920-1,000 |
| Weight (with seat) | 26.5 lbs | 27.6 lbs |
| Newborn-ready | Lie-flat seat from birth | Bassinet included |
| Second kid | Single only | Converts to double, plus a piggyback board for a third |
| The felt difference | Easy to lift into a trunk | You notice it in the parking lot |

On paper the weight gap looks like one pound. It isn't. The Vista carries bigger wheels, a bigger frame, and the bassinet hardware - pick both up in the store and your arms know. The double capability charges that tax whether you use it or not.

## Why the Cruz Won

Three reasons, in the order they actually mattered.

**The system.** We'd already picked the Mesa infant car seat - [the safest-feeling click we tried](/posts/review-uppababy-mesa/) - and everything UPPAbaby snaps into everything else. The Cruz runs the same system with one seat and one configuration to learn. First-parent brains are full enough without a convertible chassis.

**The price.** The Vista ran roughly $250 more at the time, before the double accessories you'd buy later. We put the difference toward the car seat and the accessories that actually get used daily.

**The weight.** We tried folding and lifting both in the store. You can feel the difference, and we were about to do that lift into a trunk every day for years.

## The Plan If Kid Two Came Early

The honest fear behind every "just get the Vista" recommendation: kid two arrives while kid one still rides, and now you're buying a double stroller with a single stroller's resale value to absorb.

So we made the plan explicit. If a second kid showed up on the early side, we'd sell the Cruz and buy the Vista. UPPAbaby strollers hold their resale value well - a cared-for Cruz sells for real money - so the upgrade path cost us maybe the difference of a consignment transaction, not a whole second stroller.

Kids don't arrive on the schedule you set - the thing older parents kept telling us, and the thing that shaped this whole purchase. Planning a family and scheduling a family are different activities. We bought for the kid we had coming and kept a cheap option open for a maybe.

## Four Years Later: The Plan Never Fired

Our second came later than we'd hoped - late enough that the four-year-old walks everywhere and treats the stroller as an occasional prop. The upgrade trigger never pulled.

The same Cruz we bought in 2022 now carries the infant. Same frame, same washable seat, same one-hand fold, same Mesa click. One stroller purchase, two kids, four-plus years and counting. [The full durability verdict is in the review](/posts/review-uppababy-cruz/) - short version, it's the piece of gear we'd buy again fastest.

No regrets. The upgrade plan turned out to be insurance we never needed, and insurance you don't need is cheap.

## When the Vista Is the Right Answer

The flip side, because for real families the Vista is the correct buy:

- **Twins.** Two carriers, one frame. The Cruz has no answer.
- **Two kids close together.** If kid two arrives while kid one is under two and a half, the age gap that actually rides double, the Vista earns its price back immediately.
- **You want the bassinet.** The Vista includes it; on the Cruz V2 era it was a separate purchase.
- **You'd rather buy once than manage an upgrade.** Selling and rebuying is a real errand with a real afternoon attached. Some budgets of time say pay the $250 now.

## What We'd Tell You Today

Buy for the kid you have and the nearest horizon you can actually see. If a second kid is a concrete plan inside two years - or twins are on the table - the Vista is the stroller. If a second kid is a someday, the Cruz plus a sell-and-upgrade plan gets you the same destination for less money and less weight in the trunk.

We skipped the double, ran the insurance policy, and never filed the claim. The four-year-old walks. The baby rides the same Cruz her sister rode. One purchase, two kids, and an upgrade plan we never had to use.
