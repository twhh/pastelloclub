It starts innocently enough. You make a list on your phone during a lunch break. Then you're scrolling name apps at 2am, saying "no, too popular" to one name and "no, too out there" to the next. A few weeks later you've got 47 candidates, no favorites, and a partner who keeps suggesting names you already vetoed in month two.

We've been there. Twice now. And while there's no magic formula for finding *the one*, having a sense of what other parents are actually choosing helps more than you'd think. It tells you which names will mean three Olivias in one kindergarten class, and which vintage picks are about to have their moment.

## How We Built This List

This roundup is based on the latest Social Security Administration data (the 2025 name rankings released this spring), plus the naming trends we've watched climb over the past year on name trackers and in birth announcements. Think of it as a snapshot of where girl names are headed in 2026, not a rulebook. If a name outside the top 1,000 is calling to you, answer it.

## The Top 10: Still Going Strong

These are the heavy hitters. They're popular for a reason: they sound good, they age well, and they look great on a name block shelf.

1. **Olivia** - Latin, "olive tree." A record-setting run at #1 that still manages to feel fresh and literary.
2. **Emma** - German, "whole" or "universal." Two syllables, endless nickname potential, never falls out of favor.
3. **Amelia** - German, "industrious." Has that vintage soul with a modern bounce, thanks to Amelia Earhart.
4. **Charlotte** - French, "free woman." Royal without being stuffy, with Charlie built in for free.
5. **Ava** - Latin, "life" or "bird." Short, elegant, and impossible to misspell.
6. **Sophia** - Greek, "wisdom." A global favorite that works in nearly every language.
7. **Isla** - Scottish, named for the island of Islay. Quietly stunning, and finally getting the love it deserves stateside.
8. **Mia** - Scandinavian and Italian roots, "beloved." Petite, warm, and pairs well with almost any middle name.
9. **Luna** - Latin, "moon." The celestial pick that went from quirky to mainstream in under a decade.
10. **Evelyn** - English, from Aveline, "wished-for child." Grandma-chic that somehow reads totally current.

## Names 11-25: Where It Gets Interesting

This is the sweet spot for parents who want familiar-but-not-everywhere. And this is where 2026's risers live.

11. **Eleanor** - Greek and French roots, "shining light." Intellectual, stately, with Ellie and Nora hiding inside.
12. **Harper** - English, "harp player." A modern favorite with a musical pedigree.
13. **Aria** - Italian, "air" or "melody." Lyrical without trying too hard.
14. **Nora** - Latin and Arabic roots, meaning both "honor" and "light." Short, warm, and everywhere in the best way.
15. **Penelope** - Greek, "weaver." Mythology credentials plus the world's cutest nickname, Penny.
16. **Aurora** - Latin, "dawn." Disney princess by association, celestial by meaning, and rising fast.
17. **Ellie** - English, "shining light." The nickname that graduated to a full-time first name.
18. **Camila** - Latin, "young ceremonial attendant." Melodic and international, with Cami for the diaper bag label.
19. **Gianna** - Italian, "God is gracious." GiGi as a nickname is almost too good.
20. **Violet** - Latin, from the flower. A vintage bloom sitting in the sweet spot between flowery and tailored.
21. **Hazel** - English, from the hazelnut tree. Nature name with old-Hollywood polish.
22. **Willow** - English, from the willow tree. Graceful, bendy, and unbothered, which is honestly a life goal.
23. **Maeve** - Irish, "great queen." The fastest-climbing name on this list, and it sounds like a legend.
24. **Ophelia** - Greek, "help." Shakespeare baggage and all, parents can't get enough of that O ending.
25. **Nova** - Latin, "new." A star that suddenly burns brighter, which is a pretty solid metaphor for a baby.

## The 2026 Trends Behind the List

A few patterns jump out. **Nature names keep growing**: Willow, Hazel, and Violet all climbed this year, and we expect them to keep rising. **Celestial names are having a moment**, with Luna and Nova leading a small galaxy of star-themed picks. And **vintage revivals are getting braver**: parents aren't just borrowing Grandma's name anymore, they're reaching for the great-grandma tier, which is exactly why Ophelia and Maeve are surging.

## How to Actually Pick the One

If you're stuck between a few finalists, run them through this quick filter:

- **The playground test:** Yell it like you're calling a kid in from the yard. If it feels natural, keep it.
- **The résumé test:** Picture it at the top of a job application. It should grow with her.
- **The initials check:** Write the full monogram. Save yourself the accidental acronym now.
- **The sibling test:** Say it next to your other kids' names or your top pick for a future sibling. They should be cousins, not clones.

And remember, popularity matters less than parents think. The #1 name today goes to a far smaller share of babies than the #1 name did when we were kids. Choose the one you'd happily say ten thousand times, because you will.
