Somewhere around age three, a switch flips and your kid becomes the world's most devoted fan of comedy. Not good comedy - any comedy. The knock knock joke is where every toddler starts, because it's the only joke format where you already know every line that's coming. There's a script, the kid holds half of it, and the payoff arrives whether or not anyone has explained what a pun is.

Our four-year-old currently tells roughly forty knock knock jokes a day. Her favorite one ends in "noodle." There is no punchline. It kills every time.

## The Secret Toddlers Know

Until about age four, kids don't get the wordplay, and it doesn't matter even slightly. The joke is the ritual. There's a knock, there's a mystery guest, and then - this is the part they live for - anything at all after the "...who?"

A nonsense word. An animal sound. Their own name. "Mashed potato." All of it lands. If your toddler laughs hardest at the entry with no punchline, that's developmentally on schedule; the actual pun appreciation shows up around five or six, and until then you are essentially performing call-and-response with a laugh track.

Two delivery tips from the trenches: pause after "who's there?" so the kid gets to demand the answer (owning that line is half the fun), and once in a while make one up with their name as the guest. "Knock knock. Who's there? [Kid's name]. [Kid's name] who? ...That's what I keep asking!" - instant classic, no joke required.

## The List

You know the rhythm: *knock knock, who's there, [name], [name] who?* - the punchline follows.

### The animal-sound division (toddlers' heavyweight class)

1. **Cows go** - No, silly. Cows go MOO.
2. **The interrupting cow** - MOO! (Yell it before they finish "the interrupting cow wh-" - timing is everything, and this is the one joke toddlers can beat adults at.)
3. **Cargo** - No, no. Car go BEEP BEEP.
4. **Hoo** - Are you an owl?
5. **Kanga** - No, it's *kangaroo*!

### The classics (they earned it)

6. **Boo** - Aw, don't cry, it's only a joke.
7. **Atch** - Bless you!
8. **Harry** - Harry up and open the door!
9. **Lettuce** - Lettuce in, it's freezing out here.
10. **Ice cream** - Ice cream if you don't let me in!
11. **Wooden shoe** - Wooden shoe like to hear another joke?
12. **Dishes** - Dishes the police. Open up!
13. **Annie** - Annie body home?
14. **Wendy** - Wendy you going to let me in?
15. **Luke** - Luke through the peephole and see!
16. **Tank** - You're welcome!
17. **Figs** - Figs the doorbell, it's broken!
18. **Nobel** - No bell, that's why I'm knocking!
19. **Doris** - Door is stuck, that's why I knocked.
20. **Isabel** - Isabel working? I had to knock!

### The ones for the almost-four-and-up crowd

21. **Orange** - Orange you glad I didn't say banana? (First run "banana, banana who?" twice for the full classic build-up.)
22. **Stopwatch** - Stopwatch you're doing and open this door!
23. **Canoe** - Canoe come out and play?
24. **Honey bee** - Honey bee a dear and grab that bag?
25. **Ida** - Ida who? ...I thought you said *Idaho*.

## When the Loop Never Ends

A warning, offered too late: the list runs out before the toddler does. When you've told all twenty-five and the demand continues, you have two moves. The first is nonsense - any word, any sound, delivered with confidence. The second is the reversal: hand them the setup line and let them invent the guest. Expect forty consecutive rounds of "banana," expect to laugh anyway, and know that somewhere in there, real punchlines are quietly taking root.

The day your kid gets the orange-and-banana joke unprompted is a good day. Until then, anything after the "who?" is comedy.
