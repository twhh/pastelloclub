Somewhere in a Treasury database, there is now an account with your baby's name on it. You didn't open it. You didn't fund it. And sitting inside it, for most kids born since the start of last year, is an offer of $1,000 in federal money that nobody has claimed yet.

Here's the short version of how we got here. The big tax law passed in July 2025 created Trump Accounts - starter savings accounts for kids, seeded by the government. For children born between January 1, 2025 and December 31, 2028, the federal government will deposit $1,000, once. The original signup involved filing a form with your tax return, and fewer than one in twelve eligible families did it. So in late September, the Treasury stopped waiting: it began auto-opening accounts for tens of millions of eligible kids.

Auto-opened is not the same as claimed. The $1,000 still waits on a parent to activate the account and say yes. One of our kids qualifies, so this stopped being hypothetical at our house a few weeks back - this note is everything we've learned. The claim itself runs about twenty minutes, and most of that is making sure you're on the real website.

## What the Account Actually Is

Strip away the politics and the name, and it's a simple idea: every kid in America gets a starter investment account instead of starting from zero.

The government puts in $1,000, one time, for kids born in the qualifying window. Parents can add their own money - up to $5,000 per year, from money you've already paid taxes on - but nothing about the $1,000 requires you to contribute a cent. The account grows invested over the years.

The catch, and it's a big one to understand up front: the money is locked. You can't withdraw it before the year your child turns 18, except in rare circumstances. After 18 it behaves like a retirement account your kid controls. This is not a diaper fund. It's a head start on their adulthood.

(If you're weighing this against a 529 for education money, [we wrote a whole comparison](/posts/trump-account-vs-529/) - the short version is these accounts do different jobs, and you might want both.)

## Check If Your Kid Qualifies

Three things:

- Born between January 1, 2025 and December 31, 2028 (that's the window for the $1,000; older kids under 18 can have an account, just without the federal seed)
- A U.S. citizen
- Has a Social Security number

If your baby arrived in 2025 or later, you're almost certainly in. If you're expecting, add this to the post-birth paperwork pile, right after the SSN application - because the account needs one.

## The Claim, in Four Steps

1. **Go to the official site yourself: [trumpaccounts.gov](https://www.trumpaccounts.gov).** Type it into your browser. Don't click a version of it that arrived by email or text - more on that below.
2. **Find your activation email, or wait for your phase.** Treasury has been sending activation emails in waves since the summer. Search your inbox and spam folder for it. No email yet? The portal is still the place to start; many families are activating directly.
3. **Activate the account and elect the $1,000.** Inside, you'll confirm your child's details and make what the IRS calls the election to request the one-time $1,000 pilot contribution. It's the same election as [Form 4547](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-4547), the form the early filers attached to their tax returns - the portal just walks you through it now.
4. **Decide about your own money later.** You can add up to $5,000 a year, but you can also add nothing, forever, and the $1,000 is still yours to claim. Don't let the contribution question stop the claim. Free money first, strategy second. (When you're ready to see what it could become, [our calculator projects it out to age 30](/tools/trump-account-calculator/).)

## The Scam Wave Has Already Started

Whenever the government moves money, phishers move faster. Three rules that will cover you:

- Treasury will not call, text, or DM you about your child's account.
- Nobody legitimate charges a fee to claim $1,000. Anyone selling "claim assistance" is selling you air.
- Type the web address yourself. The real portal is a .gov. Look at it before you enter anything.

A wave of fake "activate your baby's account" emails is basically guaranteed this fall. Forward anything suspicious straight to delete.

## What Happens If You Do Nothing

Honestly? Nothing dramatic. The account sits in your child's name, unactivated, waiting. The money isn't lost the day you skip it, and claiming late is possible.

But $1,000 compounding for eighteen years beats $1,000 compounding for twelve, and the whole point of the program was that families shouldn't have to be finance nerds to give their kid a start. Twenty minutes now. That's the entire ask.

## The Honest Caveats

This program is barely a year old, and pieces of it are still being written - Treasury and the IRS have been issuing guidance in stages, and future rulemaking could change details like administration and timelines. Everything in this note reflects the rules as they stand right now, and I'll update it as things firm up.

Also, plainly: I'm a parent taking notes, not a financial or tax professional, and nothing here is financial, tax, or investment advice. The links above go to the official sources, and for anything unusual about your situation - custody questions, kids born right at the edges of the window, moving abroad - a tax pro is worth the hour.

The checklist parents actually need is short. Confirm the birth year. Type the .gov. Claim the thousand. Close the laptop and go hold the baby - their first money lesson is already done.
